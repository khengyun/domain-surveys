Image
Composition

How visual elements are selected, arranged, integrated, and kept coherent across a single image—or an entire sequence.

Living survey 18 min read Updated 30 Jul 2026
Survey in one paragraph

Image composition is more than pasting one object onto another scene. A complete system must decide what to insert, where it belongs, how it should transform, what it occludes, and how its appearance should adapt to the target. For video, every decision must also remain stable through time.

01 · ScopeWhy this field exists

Four questions define the problem.

Different papers solve different slices of composition. Reading them as one field is easier when each method is located by the decision it owns: the operator, the spatial fit, the appearance match, or the temporal extension.

What is combined?

Pixels, layers, objects, identities, latent features, or complete generated regions.

Where does it fit?

Location, scale, support, perspective, pose, depth, and semantic compatibility.

How does it become believable?

Boundary refinement, color, illumination, texture, blur, noise, shadow, and reflection.

What changes in video?

Trajectories, masks, motion blur, camera noise, and occlusion must agree over time.

04 · Reading pathsEditorial scaffold

Turn a bibliography into decisions.

A useful survey should explain why a reader would choose one path over another. This starter structure is intentionally method-oriented; add verified papers and links as the review develops.

Start here

  • Define the composition operator and the expected form of control.
  • Separate geometric plausibility from photometric realism.

For object insertion

  • Read placement and transformation before blending.
  • Check whether masks, depth, support relations, or reference poses are required.

For realism

  • Compare boundary quality, global harmonization, and illumination consistency.
  • Inspect shadow and reflection behavior separately from color matching.

For video

  • Track identity, shape, and occlusion—not only pixel similarity.
  • Report failure under camera motion, motion blur, and long occlusion.

This is a living map: its value comes from making boundaries visible, then revising them when the field no longer fits.
